# Saturn protocol vision and build specification

**Design baseline 1.3 · 26 November 2026**

Saturn lets a creator launch a capped-supply token into an XLM market without supplying the initial XLM liquidity. Buyers fund a redeemable bonding curve. At a published inventory threshold, its XLM reserve and a separate token allocation become permanent base liquidity in a constant-product pool. Traders use one swap screen. Traders who want price control can post orders on Saturn’s STRN/XLM and MOON/XLM books.

STRN stakers earn STRN, MOON and XLM. MOON stakers earn MOON and retain utility access. Each project’s stakers earn its own token. A burn restores permission to mint that asset as a future staking reward. Real XLM paid by traders funds the XLM rewards. The owner governs settings in v1. Tokenholder voting would require a separate governance decision.

**Decision record.** STRN and MOON transfer fees start at 1% each and governance may set either anywhere from 0% to 1%. At genesis the Foundation receives 16M STRN, plans to offer 10M of that inventory on the DEX, and 5M remains unissued for staking. Community buyers and liquidity providers fund the DEX. Launchpad tokens reserve 30% of supply for ten years of initial staking rewards. Burns fund additional issuance. Both scheduled and recycled MOON are split equally between the STRN and MOON staking pools. The orderbook’s 25/25/50 distribution applies to its XLM half. The creator/Foundation/STRN-staker split applies on project venues. Core AMMs use the 50/25/12.5/12.5 split in section 3. Hybrid rent management is approved. The external-pool residual, LP fee denomination, initial offer prices and other open parameters are identified below.

**Supply accounting.** For every Saturn-issued token, enforce the integer identity **outstanding + unused initial reward capacity + unused recycled reward capacity = cap**. Outstanding is the sum of all token balances, including escrow, bonded stake, inventory and minted but unclaimed rewards. Free float is a separate disclosure with a published exclusion policy. Mintable capacity is an accounting limit, never a transferable balance or collateral. A qualifying burn atomically reduces outstanding and adds the same units to recycled capacity. An authorized reward mint debits exactly one source of capacity and adds exactly those units to outstanding. Lifetime gross minting can exceed the cap through recycling. Outstanding cannot.

Delivery of an already-minted claim is a transfer.

At full placement and before fills, 10M is escrowed for those orders, 6M remains in the Foundation wallet and 5M stays unissued for staking. Posting moves existing inventory and does not change outstanding supply. Escrowed STRN cannot earn staking rewards.

1. The Foundation signs one or more priced asks totaling 10M STRN, using scoped maker escrow. Publish the opening price or price ladder and any staging before accepting buys. A sell order can be posted with STRN inventory alone. The transaction fee account still needs XLM for network fees and rent.
2. A buyer supplies XLM as an ask fills. XLM principal is credited to the Foundation as seller. 3. This opens a market for purchases. A holder can sell on the book when a community participant posts a funded XLM bid. For the STRN/XLM AMM, the first LP must supply both assets. Community participants can buy STRN and then deposit STRN and XLM. LP shares belong to contributors under section 6. An orderbook fill creates no LP shares and does not automatically deposit sale proceeds into an AMM. Any Foundation contribution of proceeds is a separate, disclosed action.
4. Enable STRN staking as buyers acquire tokens. The first positive STRN staking epoch can unlock the first MOON issuance, funding both MOON beneficiaries equally. Claimed MOON can supply MOON/XLM asks and, together with XLM, community LP deposits.

Saturn’s book and pool are separate contracts. The initial STRN distribution uses its orderbook; the bonding-curve graduation rules in section 5 apply to launchpad tokens. **Stellar boundary.** A custom Soroban token address is a different asset identifier from a classic issued asset. Saturn’s Soroban orderbook uses a different contract ID on each side of a trade. The entire fill succeeds or rolls back. A MOON ask substitutes the MOON contract for STRN. Neither token needs a SAC, a classic issuer or a wrapped representation. ## 2. Transfer fees, allowances and settlement

Core transfer fees start at 100 bps for STRN and MOON independently. Governance can increase or decrease either rate to any integer from 0 to 100 bps inclusive, including a later increase from zero. The token contract itself enforces the maximum. On a taxable gross transfer g in atomic units burn floor(g × rate_bps / 10,000) and credit the recipient with the rest. Debit exactly g with one authorization, including for transfer_from. A 100 STRN transfer at 1% delivers 99 STRN and creates 1 STRN of recycled capacity. MOON follows the same rule in MOON units. At 0 bps, delivery is exact and the transfer creates no reward capacity. Omit zero burn events. Seven decimals are proposed for Saturn tokens. Positive self-transfers are rejected. Small transfers whose fee rounds to zero create no issuance credit.

**Token interfaces.** Implement standard holder and delegated burns and the same recycle accounting for these, ordinary transfer-fee burns and authorized venue-fee burns. Emit a burn event for the actual quantity destroyed, a transfer event for the amount delivered (preserve muxed recipient data where applicable), and distinct events for capacity source, reward mint and claim. The standard transfer signature does not promise that other integrations understand a net-of-fee receipt. Document the core assets’ custom transfer semantics and test supported wallets. Ordinary project transfers deliver the exact requested amount. [SEP-41 interface and events](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/ecosystem/sep-0041.md)

**Authority boundary.** Cap, burn accounting and the reward-only mint authority are immutable. Each token enforces both source balances locally. Approved fee venues can burn only an authenticated payer’s fee or the precise trader-funded fee in their own custody. A holder burn confers no preferential claim on future rewards. No sink contract, treasury reserve withdrawal or owner mint is needed. The separate LP locker continues to hold genesis liquidity shares.

| Movement | Transfer-fee rule | Settlement rule |
|---|---|---|
| Wallet payment or ordinary delegated transfer | 0–1% of gross, at the current token rate | Delegated spender needs a live allowance for the gross debit and consumes exactly that amount. Approval itself moves no funds. |
| Maker escrow, taker settlement, cancellations and refunds | Exempt through the registered book’s scoped settlement method | Reserve and release exact amounts. Refund only the recorded owner. A direct transfer into the book creates no order or withdrawable credit. |
| Pool deposits, withdrawals and swaps | Exempt through the registered pool’s scoped method | Book reserves from verified asset movements. Send withdrawals to the LP owner and swap outputs to the signed trader. |
| Stake, unstake, reward vault funding and claim | Exempt through the registered vault method | Credit principal and claims exactly to their owner. Reward minting at epoch settlement spends the budget. A claim delivers previously minted tokens and creates no fee loop. |
| Creator payment and ordinary project-token trading | XLM and ordinary project-token transfers carry no core-token tax | Deduct the published creation or trading fee separately. |
| Router, outside venues, approved accounts | Router has no blanket exemption. Outside venues use ordinary transfers by default. | An account exemption applies only to a registered direction and named counterparty. Display its scope and expiry. Third-party pools must support net receipts or use an approved integration. |

**How exemptions work on-chain.** Both ordinary transfer entry points use the identical tax rule. Implement a separate token method for exempt movements taking venue, action, from, to, amount and policy version. Check an enabled grant for that venue and action, require venue authorization and, for user debits, the user’s exact authorization. Validate custody endpoint and direction. The venue proves the economic reason from its own records. Approve only immutable or version-pinned venue logic with owner-bound refunds and withdrawals, no transferable escrow credits and no generic forwarding. Account-to-account exemptions require the exact registered pair to sign or authorize their designated roles as applicable. Never infer an exemption from the transaction source, router or an address merely appearing in the call stack. Governance can onboard and sunset grants. On revocation disable new activity and preserve narrowly scoped existing-principal exits at original terms.

## 3. Trading fees in tokens and XLM

Use the trade’s actual execution quantities to split fee value. For a book fill or curve trade let Q be gross base-token principal, N its gross XLM principal and r the venue fee. Before rounding, F_token = Q × r / 2 and F_XLM = N × r / 2. Charge the taker in both assets at the fill’s own average execution price. No token/XLM oracle or conversion trade is needed. For a **buy**, pay N + F_XLM and receive Q − F_token. For a **sell**, provide Q + F_token and receive N − F_XLM. The maker always exchanges exactly Q for N, plus its separate rebate. Exact-input sells must solve for Q within the signed gross token budget. Apply the same wallet convention to graduated and core AMMs. A scoped venue settlement avoids an additional core wallet transfer charge on settlement, LP contributions, refunds, and fee collection.

Use native precision per asset, integer checked products and a common basis-point denominator. Proposed rounding: floor each gross fee leg once per fill, require a published minimum on both legs when the rate is positive, and reject a fill below that minimum or with zero net output. For project and core AMMs retain floor(half) of each fee leg for LPs and route the remainder to burn or the XLM beneficiary bucket. For each 25/25/50 XLM bucket allocate full groups of four stroops and carry a cursor through [stakers, payee A, stakers, Foundation] for residual stroops. Payee A is the resting maker on the book and the creator on a project. Key the cursor by venue and entitled payee. On a core AMM, divide its remaining XLM bucket equally between Foundation and STRN stakers. Alternate the recipient of any odd stroop using one persistent cursor per pool. Round external input fees once, use cumulative 40/60 accounting for fractional Foundation units, and retain the rest for LPs. Display both fee quantities, net settlement, price impact, bounds and separate network charges.

| Source | Starting charge | Distribution of the charge |
|---|---:|---|
| Creation | 100 XLM per successful launch | 100% to operations, initially earmarked for launch and rent support |
| STRN/XLM or MOON/XLM orderbook | 1% taker, 0% maker | Burn the token half. Split the XLM half 25% resting maker, 25% Saturn Foundation and 50% STRN stakers. |
| Project bonding curve | 1% | Burn the project-token half. Split the XLM half 25% creator, 25% Foundation and 50% STRN stakers. |
| Graduated project AMM | 1% | LPs earn half the whole fee. Of the whole fee, another quarter is burned in project tokens and a quarter is XLM split 25% creator, 25% Foundation and 50% STRN stakers. Proposed LP settlement retains half of each asset leg in reserves. |
| External Stellar token/XLM AMM | 0.5% | 40% of the fee to the Foundation. Proposed remaining 60% to LPs. Charge the input asset and transfer the Foundation share in kind. The external issuer retains its asset’s issuance policy. |
| STRN/XLM or MOON/XLM AMM | 1% | Of the total trading fee, 50% goes to LPs, 25% is burned in the traded token, 12.5% goes to the Saturn Foundation in XLM and 12.5% to STRN stakers in XLM. Under the proposed LP denomination, LPs retain half of each asset’s fee leg. |

**Example in native units.** At 2 XLM per token, Q = 50 tokens and N = 100 XLM. A 1% venue charges **0.25 token + 0.50 XLM**, worth 1 XLM together. The buyer pays 100.50 XLM and receives 49.75 tokens. In the reverse-direction example the seller provides 50.25 tokens and receives 99.50 XLM. Both use the same gross 50-token execution.

| Venue at this execution | Token fee (0.25 token) | XLM fee (0.50 XLM) |
|---|---|---|
| Book | Burn 0.25 | Maker 0.125, Foundation 0.125, STRN stakers 0.25 |
| Curve | Burn 0.25 | Creator 0.125, Foundation 0.125, STRN stakers 0.25 |
| Graduated AMM | LP reserves 0.125, burn 0.125 | LP reserves 0.25, creator 0.0625, Foundation 0.0625, STRN stakers 0.125 |
| STRN/XLM or MOON/XLM AMM | LP reserves 0.125, burn 0.125 | LP reserves 0.25, Foundation 0.125, STRN stakers 0.125 |

On a 100 XLM gross input into an external AMM, the fee is 0.50 XLM. The Foundation gets 0.20 XLM and LPs would retain 0.30 XLM under the proposed residual. A token-input trade pays the equivalent percentages in that input token.

Each fill, burn, actual asset movement and beneficiary credit commits atomically. A canceled or reverted order creates neither revenue nor capacity. Credit the creator address pinned in the launch record. Authenticated payout address rotation affects future earnings only. Track maker and creator credits by entitled owner. Escrowed principal and AMM seed inventory cannot pay rebates or rewards. An empty staking pool’s assigned share remains earmarked for its beneficiaries.

## 4. Stake power and reward distribution

STRN, MOON and each launchpad token have separate staking pools. Keep one nontransferable position per account per pool. For each finalized reward day, an account’s share of every distributable asset in its pool equals its stake power divided by that pool’s total stake power. STRN uses the same share for all three assets. MOON uses its own power denominator for MOON rewards, independently of its utility threshold. A project uses its own denominator for its project token.

**Proposed power curve.** Use daily UTC epochs and a 180-day loyalty cap H. Each token contributes one unit of base power and earns up to one additional unit linearly over H days. For principal B and credited age A in days, power is B × (1 + A/H). Retain fractional age in the calculation and round only for display. At 0, 45, 90 and 180 days each token has 1.00, 1.25, 1.50 and 2.00 units of power. The cap limits the advantage a founding staker can accumulate over later entrants.

**Adding or removing stake.** Keep age credit C in atomic-token-days. Each full active day adds B to C up to H × B. At a top-up of d, keep the existing credit and increase principal to B + d. The new credited age is C/(B+d). At a partial withdrawal of w, keep floor(C × (B−w)/B) credit and B−w principal. A full exit clears age credit. Claims do not reset age. Mature stake stops accumulating credit at 180 days, so past time above the cap cannot give a fresh deposit an instant bonus. This is a balance-weighted *credited* age. Wallet history can separately report the original deposit dates.

| Example at the effective boundary | Active stake | Credited age | Stake power |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before a deposit | 100 | 90 days | 150 |
| Immediately after adding 100 | 200 | 45 days | 250 |
| After a further 45 days | 200 | 90 days | 300 |
| After withdrawing 50 at that point | 150 | 90 days | 225 |

**Which day earns what.** The ledger timestamp determines the UTC day. An intent submitted in day e takes effect at the start of e+1. Deposits are escrowed immediately and earn from that next boundary. Withdrawals remain committed to the pool through the current day and become collectible at the next boundary. Multiple intents for a boundary use one deterministic transition: age the existing balance, process pending exits pro rata, then add new principal at age zero. Pending deposits can be canceled before activation. A withdrawal may address only active principal and cannot be collected twice. A boundary-effective deposit only participates in fees and emissions of its new day. Display effective time, projected power and earliest withdrawal before signing. This makes same-transaction staking for a single fill uneconomic and leaves up to one day of exit latency.

**Daily accounting without user sweeps.** Use P = H × B + C internally. The common H denominator cancels in reward shares. Persist the pool’s total P at each epoch, each asset’s daily budget and position changes effective that epoch. A position with fixed B has a predictable future sequence: P grows by B per day, takes a last fractional increment at the cap if needed, then stays constant. Aggregate these ramp changes by future epoch in a sparse schedule. On a stake intent replace only that owner’s previously scheduled future contribution from the effective boundary onward. The indexer can project the result; the contract is authoritative. At rollover apply scheduled changes in bounded batches of days, with permissionless catch-up and a persistent cursor. Charge fees to their actual ledger day even during catch-up. Pay claims only for finalized epochs. Apply every missed transition in order before an action needing current totals. A keeper outage delays processing without changing entitlements.

For asset budget D and vault total P_total, accrue the account’s D × P_account / P_total. Use checked wide math with high-precision fractional carry, retain unassigned dust for that reward stream, and record each owner’s claim cursor to prevent duplicate claims. Store position checkpoints and epoch sums persistently so an account can exit without first replaying years of claims. Cap each catch-up and claim call by a measured number of days, resume permissionlessly, and allow payment only to the owner. Benchmark long-dormant accounts. Test global power against an independent sum of account powers around boundary timestamps and cap transitions.

| Reward stream | Source and duration |
|---|---|
| STRN stake → STRN | Release the initial 5M plus recycled STRN from all eligible STRN burns. Testnet proposal: spread the initial allocation over 2,920 positive-power days (eight 365-day years), averaging 625,000 per eligible year. |
| STRN and MOON stake → MOON | Release initial MOON capacity over a proposed 3,650 eligible days and recycled MOON on its separate schedule. Split both sources 50/50 between the two pools at mint time. MOON stake also grants utility after the proposed 24-hour qualification at 1,000 MOON. |
| STRN stake → XLM | Receive 50% of the applicable XLM distribution bucket on book, curve and graduated AMM trades. Credit only realized XLM, distributed by the STRN pool’s daily power. Core AMMs also pay STRN stakers 12.5% of the entire trading fee in XLM. |
| Launchpad token stake → its own token | Reserve 300M unissued units out of the 1B cap. Release them linearly over 3,650 positive-power days (ten 365-day years), averaging 30M per eligible year. This budget covers the full schedule even at zero trading volume. Trading-fee and voluntary burns create additional same-token reward capacity. XLM staking fees on these venues go to STRN stakers. |

**After the initial allocation.** Project founders and community members have a decade of scheduled issuance to build uses that consume their token. For example, an app can let a holder authorize burning tokens for access to a project service. Use the token’s canonical burn or authorized burn_from path so each unit burned credits recycled capacity exactly once. A transfer to a creator or treasury is a payment and generates no burn credit. Trading-fee burns and community-initiated burns can fund extra rewards during the ten years and continue afterward. Once the initial budget and previously accrued recycled capacity are spent, further staking issuance depends on new burns. The initial release pauses on days without eligible stake, so its calendar end can fall later than the tenth anniversary.

**Release algorithm.** On eligible day n with horizon T and initial allocation I, release the difference between floor(I × min(n,T)/T) and its prior cumulative target. Use the same eligible-day clock for the two halves of MOON and start it when either pool has positive power. For each asset, recycled units burned on a ledger day mature no earlier than the next eligible day. Recommended smoothing spreads that cohort across 30 eligible days by the same cumulative-difference rule, regardless of the initial release schedule. A burn never accelerates initial issuance. Aggregate burns by asset and UTC day inside the token, reconcile by authenticated source, and consume each day’s scheduled release once. Bounded catch-up is required. A straggling keeper cannot mint the same missed day twice.

**Payout lifecycle.** The reward controller finalizes an epoch and calls a token method that enforces the schedules, funding source and authorized vault destinations. STRN and each project token mint into their own reward escrow. MOON atomically mints equal amounts into both reward escrows. Carry an odd atomic unit forward within its original issuance source until it can form a pair. Expose the rounding carry and define a terminal unmatched unit as unissued until a later unit in the same source pairs with it. A user claim advances each selected asset’s cursor and transfers already-escrowed rewards. Each pool keeps its staked principal segregated from reward reserves, including when the staked token and reward token are identical. No reward transfer triggers a transfer-fee burn inside this authorized path.

**Example epoch.** Suppose the STRN pool receives 1,700 STRN from initial capacity plus 300 STRN from recycling, 10,000 MOON as its half of a 20,000 MOON issue, and 50 XLM of realized fees. A staker with 2% of that pool’s power earns **40 STRN, 200 MOON and 1 XLM**. The other 10,000 MOON belong to the MOON pool and use that pool’s power denominator. These example amounts illustrate entitlement, not forecast revenue.

**Empty pools.** Pause initial and recycled release clocks for STRN or a project when its only beneficiary pool has zero power. For MOON, pause both clocks only when both pools are empty. If just one side is empty, mint equal amounts and hold that side’s share in its segregated carryover. Never divert it to the other pool. Likewise hold collected XLM on zero-power STRN days. Proposed policy releases a consecutive empty period’s balance over the next 30 positive-power days of its beneficiary pool, pausing installments on later empty days. Track these as already-funded carryovers, separate from unused mint capacity. A sole eligible account can earn the full distribution for its pool on a given day. Test concentration at startup and after inactivity.

## 5. Launch lifecycle and bonding curve

**Create → Curve trading → Ready to graduate → AMM trading.** A signed creator pays the current XLM fee up to their supplied ceiling. The factory creates the named token, curve and staking vault from approved hashes and assigns issued inventory and reserves the staking mint budget before exposing a live market. Creator names and symbols may collide. Use contract IDs as identity and validate and bound metadata. The creator receives no discretionary mint, reserve withdrawal or price-setting authority. Creators can acquire tokens through the same market.

| Genesis destination | Tokens | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Curve inventory S | 400,000,000 | Public sale and pre-graduation redemptions |
| AMM seed inventory B | 300,000,000 | Segregated until graduation |
| Initial staking mint budget | 300,000,000 | Ten-year initial reward capacity for launchpad token stakers |
| **Total cap** | **1,000,000,000** | **700M issued at genesis plus 300M reserved for staking** |

Let s be net units sold by the curve, between zero and S. Tokens are fungible. Bound sells by s and the actual reserve even if the seller earned the tokens through staking. Proposed marginal price in XLM per whole token is p(s) = p₀ + (p₁ − p₀)s/S, with p₀ = 0.00005 and p₁ = 0.00010. The XLM liability is R(s) = p₀s + (p₁ − p₀)s²/(2S). The curve starts with R(0) = 0. Real XLM paid by buyers funds redemptions.

**Executable pricing.** Implement a single cumulative function C(s) = floor(R(s) in stroops), using rational coefficients and atomic token units. A buy of gross q has XLM principal C(s+q) − C(s), adds q to s, and sends q less the token burn to the buyer. A sell of gross principal q has XLM principal C(s) − C(s−q), subtracts q from s, returns exactly q to inventory, and charges the seller’s token burn on top of q. Apply the separate XLM fee from section 3 to wallet settlement. This makes principal telescope across trades and leaves recorded reserve equal to C(s). Reject zero notional, trades below the minimum and zero net proceeds. For an exact-input buy, use bounded integer search over size or verify a quoted size and its upper neighbor. Limit to remaining inventory, charge only consumed XLM and require explicit partial-fill consent. Selling to zero returns the curve to its initial state. Trading halts at s = S.

**Worked graduation.** At 200M sold the marginal price is 0.000075 and cumulative reserve is 12,500 XLM. At 400M sold the reserve is 30,000 XLM. Pairing that reserve with 300M seed tokens opens the pool at 30,000 / 300,000,000 = **0.00010 XLM per token**. With 30% reserved as staking capacity, opening XLM depth is 30,000. The earlier 10% staking template modeled 40,000. The same order moves the opening market more. Model price impact and LP participation across realistic trade sizes before selecting the template. Fees, donations and staking capacity are excluded. The marginal pool price matches the terminal curve price before venue fees and slippage. A future template must satisfy C(S)/B ≈ p₁ to within a published rounding tolerance.

**Graduation custody.** Final fill marks the launch ready in the same transaction. Any caller can finalize. Deploy or access the unique factory-reserved empty pool, transfer both tracked reserves, initialize shares, send all genesis LP shares to an immutable LP locker, and mark live in one atomic finalization. The locker can never redeem or transfer. A failed finalization leaves both assets in escrow, accepts no further curve trades, and permits a retry. Register the seed owner and ceremony in the pool constructor. Unsolicited transfers cannot set the initial price or block initialization. Publish the factory version and resource-test finalization before enabling a template. A stalled sale below threshold remains redeemable at the curve quote with no owner sweep or forced graduation.

## 6. AMM, orderbook and unified swaps

**AMM pricing and retained fees.** In a Saturn-token/XLM pool let x and y be tracked XLM and token reserves. For a buy of XLM pricing principal a, gross token output Q = floor(y × a/(x+a)); principal N=a. For a sell of pricing principal b tokens, gross XLM output N = floor(x × b/(y+b)); principal Q=b. Use the dual-asset fee and taker settlement from section 3. On a buy the reserves end at (x + N + LP_XLM, y − Q + LP_token). On a sell they end at (x − N + LP_XLM, y + Q + LP_token). The non-LP token fee is burned and the non-LP XLM fee is paid or segregated as a liability outside reserves. The LP portions accrue in both reserve assets. Trading fees never come out of the permanent LP locker.

For a proposed external-pool gross input g, charge f = floor(g × 50/10,000) in the input asset. Price only g−f against the invariant, retain the LP share of f in input reserves and send the Foundation its share. Apply the formula in either direction with the corresponding asset precision. For every swap, test the post-trade product against the pre-trade product. Quote and execution must use the same integer arithmetic. V1 external listings are XLM pairs with verified asset ID, decimals, transfer behavior and custody permissions. Admit ordinary exact-delivery assets first. Handle issuer authorization, transfer restrictions or changing asset behavior through listing review, accurate receipts and fail-closed settlement. Saturn has no reward mint authority over external tokens. [Stellar Asset Contract](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/stellar-asset-contract)

Issue initial LP shares proportional to floor(sqrt(x × y)) in atomic units. For later deposits mint min(floor(dx × L / x), floor(dy × L / y)) shares, where L is outstanding shares. Take only the corresponding proportional amounts rounded up within the signer’s maxima. On withdrawal burn shares and release each reserve pro rata rounded down, enforcing minimum outputs, positive economic size and permanent locked liquidity. Fees compound in reserves. User LP shares in core pools and graduated pools represent their own deposits. For externally seeded core pools, permanently lock a small disclosed minimum share amount and assign remaining seed shares to the actual contributors. Keep unsolicited surplus outside pricing, reserve ownership and share issuance. No permissionless skim of principal.

**Orderbook.** Use one contract per base-token/XLM pair. Store fully escrowed bids in XLM and asks in base tokens. Use integer price ticks and base lots so that a lot at any tick has an integral XLM-stroop value. Proposed initial lot of one whole core token and tick of one stroop per lot, minimum fill 0.1 XLM except the cancelable dust remainder. Confirm tick/lot fit from MOON price discovery before enabling that market. Use a finite tick domain with a bounded hierarchical occupancy bitmap, and FIFO queues of monotonic order IDs per price. Best executable ask is the lowest tick, best executable bid the highest. Price and remaining quantity are checked on-chain.

- Post-only orders that would cross are rejected. An immediate-or-cancel taker consumes only resting liquidity at resting prices. A marketable limit is a taker for the matched portion. A residual can be posted via a separate post-only instruction after a fresh quote. Reducing or repricing an order uses cancel and replace with a new queue position.
- Match in strict price/time order, including partial fills. Publish a measured cap, starting testnet target eight resting orders per call. Stop or revert according to the signed partial-fill policy at that cap. Bounded prune calls remove expired heads and release escrow to owner credit. Stale heads cannot be skipped to obtain a worse price. Trading deadlines and order expiry are independent of storage TTL.
- The maker may cancel unfilled size or withdraw settled credit while placement is paused. An order race resolves by ledger execution order. Reject direct self-matches against the same owner. The rebate is a fraction of paid fees, and aggregate maker plus staker payouts are bounded by receipts. Related accounts can still trade with one another. Track concentration and incentive recycling, and issue no volume-based subsidies.
- Partial fill example. An ask for 50 STRN at 2 XLM sells 20 STRN gross. The buyer pays 40.20 XLM and receives 19.90 STRN. Burn 0.10 STRN. The seller receives 40 XLM principal plus 0.05 XLM rebate. The Foundation receives 0.05 XLM and STRN stakers receive 0.10 XLM. The remaining 30 STRN stays in escrow.

**Swap interface and router.** Quote eligible routes from the same closed ledger: curve before graduation, book and/or pool where live afterwards. Compare executable recipient output at the same gross wallet budget, incorporating trade fees, transfer exemptions, lot rounding, depth, slippage and leftovers. Show venue, price impact, expected net output, fee breakdown, resource estimate, quote ledger and expiry. A stale indexer cannot authorize settlement. Re-simulate the selected transaction before signing.

MVP uses one venue and exact-input swaps. v1 can split one pair’s trade across book and pool atomically where simulation shows more output after fees and acceptable resource cost. Use bounded, enumerated legs, no arbitrary targets and no router custody between transactions. Bind network, addresses, venue/config versions, recipient, input cap, deadline, minimum net output and partial-fill choice to the user’s authorization. Send proceeds back to the signed trader in v1. The router must check the final aggregate receipt and any unspent input. Revert the entire swap if any signed bound fails. Offer a fresh quote after a race or graduation. Direct venue entry points apply identical bounds.

## 7. Contract boundaries, data and authority

| Contract or service | Principal entry points and calls | State, authority and events |
|---|---|---|
| Governance and registry | propose, cancel, execute, pause, register_template, register_market. Dispatch typed setters to named modules. | Owner multisig. Persist action hash, network/domain, nonce, ETA, target and version. Emit proposed, executed, canceled, paused, ownership and registry changes. |
| STRN, MOON, project token and rewards controller | Token ABI, scoped transfer, set_transfer_fee_bps (core only), burn, burn_from, epoch settlement, supply_state. Emit transfer, burn, capacity credited, reward mint and fee change with old/new rates and policy version. |
| Foundation custody and opening orders | Foundation multisig receives 16M at genesis and places priced asks from its own balance using the ordinary book entry point. Target opening inventory is 10M. | Publish the custody address, ask prices and order IDs. Reconcile free Foundation STRN, unfilled order escrow, filled quantity, XLM proceeds, maker rebates and Foundation fee income separately. |
| Launch factory and project token | create_launch(creator, metadata, salt, max_fee), resolve_launch. Emit launch address, template hash, inventory, price parameters and fee version. |
| Curve and pool factory | quote_buy/sell, buy/sell, graduate. Curve calls pool factory and transfers XLM, seed inventory and LP ownership. | One-way phase, sold quantity, separate accounted reserve and seed. Emit trades, ready and graduation with both reserves and pool ID. |

| Fee accounting and operations treasury | Compiled common split logic in venues. Venue atomically pays vaults and the applicable Foundation or operations account, pays or credits creators and makers, and records the token fee burn. | No general-purpose custodial fee distributor. | Router, indexer and maintenance | Router calls only registered venue ABIs. Indexer builds book depth, holder history and launch views. External keeper submits unsigned-by-owner maintenance or epoch rollover calls using its own funded transaction account. | Router stores small registry configuration. Indexer retains raw events and checkpoints. Keepers have no market, mint or withdrawal authority. |

**Storage layout.** Instance storage holds small bounded configuration and hot per-instance scalars. Persistent keys hold any growing owner/order/market record and recoverable financial rights. Allowances may be temporary only if their full logical validity is covered by the key TTL and expiration means zero; persistent allowances with an explicit ledger expiry are simpler for v1. Use temporary storage only for disposable caches or hints. Never store escrow, claim status, proposal execution protection or spendable credit exclusively in temporary entries. Version keys and event payloads. Use contract ID, ledger, transaction hash and event index to deduplicate. Indexers backfill from a retained ledger/event archive, compare state and reconcile balances before serving quotes. [Stellar storage](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/contract-development/storage/state-archival) · [Contract events](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/events/consume)

**Authorization and deployment.** User operations bind the owner and amounts with Soroban authorization. A registry entry grants discovery only. Each target independently enforces caller authority, capability, local bounds and expected version. Cross-contract token debits use a narrowly authorized invocation tree. Validate token IDs, network, registration and factory provenance. Treat receipts as spendable only after the transfer succeeds. Errors unwind the transaction. Use deterministic deployment salts, pinned WASM hashes and atomic constructors. Precompute all token, reward controller and vault addresses from the deployer and distinct salts. Verify immutable destinations and authorities in both directions before enabling markets. Prevent partially initialized bundles from accepting funds. [Cross-contract calls and constructors](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/contract-development/contract-interactions/cross-contract)

**Governance policy.** Proposed owner is a 2-of-3 Stellar multisig, with separate operators. Require a 48-hour timelock for fee changes, grants, future templates and rotation, and two-step acceptance for a new owner. Hard-code initial envelopes in target modules: book and curve fees 0–200 bps, AMM 0–100 bps and creation 0–1,000 XLM. Enforce sum 10,000 bps within each distribution bucket and the venue’s registered fee class. The book’s 25/25/50 split applies only to its XLM half. Each core token enforces 0 ≤ new_transfer_bps ≤ 100 on execution, with version checks and an event. Either direction is permitted, including re-enabling a fee after setting it to zero. Moving one token’s rate leaves the other’s rate unchanged. Venue fees are independently versioned. A zero transfer fee leaves venue burns, voluntary burns and already-earned rewards intact. Freeze curve coefficients, allocations and graduation rules per launch. Parameter changes affect future executions only. Order placement can pin a minimum maker rebate or opt into the posted schedule; if an update makes an order ineligible, allow permissionless cancellation to owner credit. Version signed taker quotes. Default immutable v1 custody modules, versioned replacements for new markets and voluntary user exit/migration. The guardian can immediately pause new risk, and timelocked governance resumes it. Preserve cancellation, claims and principal withdrawals wherever solvent. No owner rescue path for user reserves, locked LP shares or any staking mint capacity.

**Failure handling.** Return stable errors for deadline, slippage, changed version, insufficient allowance, unknown asset, illiquid market, invalid phase, expired order, zero net amount and resource-bound requests. Show actionable wallet messages and require fresh authorization if bounds change. Reconcile each contract’s asset balance against the sum of its liabilities and reserves, tracking donations as surplus. Asset operations fail closed on a mismatch. A graduate or claim may be retried after failure without a second payout. Publish verified contract IDs, hashes, ABI and event schemas for each network.

## 8. Keeping Soroban contracts and state available

| Approach | Where it earns its cost | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit bump on access | On successful state changes, extend low-TTL entries already touched and the active instance/code where appropriate. | Dormant markets, old orders, cold claims and shared dependencies may receive no traffic. |
| Governance-funded external keeper | Prepay extensions of shared WASM, critical instances, live books/curves, vault indices and eligible priority keys. Batch keys by footprint and fee budget. | Requires monitoring, funded transaction accounts and a recoverable key inventory. Governance itself cannot schedule execution. |
| Permissionless extension and restoration | Any participant can pay for a targeted protocol-level extension. Wallets can simulate and restore a cold claim or cancel an archived order. | A caller may bear rent and need a separate restore transaction when an invocation exceeds resource limits. |

**Approved operating model.** Saturn pays to keep shared contracts available through a funded maintenance service. Active markets renew important records as people use them. When someone returns after a long absence, the app detects archived records, prepares their restoration and shows any fee to the signer. A persistent stake, order balance or reward entitlement survives archival. The treasury and keepers need no authority over user funds. Use the three mechanisms together. Track instance keys by contract ID, code keys by hash and persistent entries individually by ledger key. Deduplicate shared code across factory deployments. Index creation, order and stake events to rebuild the inventory. Read live-until ledgers from RPC, use the network’s current maximum and estimate fees from simulation. Normal reads and writes alone do not renew TTL. Extending an instance and its WASM does not extend its user balances. Temporary entries disappear on expiry. Archived persistent entries retain rights and must be restored. Since Protocol 23 an invocation can auto-restore entries declared in its restore list, which simulation normally supplies. [Stellar state archival and restoration](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/contract-development/storage/state-archival)

**Operating policy.** Start testnet with a keeper scan every six hours, a low-water threshold of 25% of the network maximum TTL and a target of 75%. Express values in ledgers; compute wall-clock estimates for dashboards only. Maintain a treasury-funded, daily-capped fee account with a second independent keeper and alerts for missed scans, approaching expiry, RPC lag and less than 30 days’ estimated runway. Size the reserve from the measured cost of renewing shared code and active state, restoring cold keys, and a two-times stress margin. Use designated creation fees plus committed seed funding until volume covers costs. Foundation contributions require a separate budget decision. The keeper spends the transaction fee account’s XLM. Do not reimburse arbitrary callers from reported costs. Participants can sponsor their own renewals.

**Recovery procedure.** Simulate against a recent closed ledger and assemble the supplied footprint and restore list. If combined restore and business execution exceeds limits, sponsor a separate bounded RestoreFootprint transaction, confirm it, re-simulate and execute with fresh bounds. Restore the instance, WASM and relevant data as needed. Re-extend low-TTL restored entries. Keep explicit expiry semantics intact. A restored expired order can only release escrow. Monitor failed fee funding, publish downtime and keep direct user exits available through an independent RPC. Rehearse a cold withdrawal and a graduation with a deliberately stopped keeper. [Stellar restoration guidance](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/cli/cookbook/restore-contract-storage)

## 10. Design review and decisions before deployment

| Finding | Design consequence and release evidence |
|---|---|
| Burn-to-mint recycles tokens | Burning creates future issuance authority. It creates no XLM or outside value. Wallets should show cap, outstanding, burned this epoch, reissued this epoch, and initial/recycled budgets. A net decrease in outstanding depends on burns exceeding mints over the period. |
| Fees and supply are distinct ledgers | Verify each buy, sell, partial fill, voluntary burn, taxable payment, exempt settlement and reward claim. Run conservation checks per asset and per funding source. Show the two fees in the wallet before signing. |
| MOON starts at zero | Allow initial issuance when STRN staking alone is active and escrow the empty MOON pool’s half. Verify a user can claim MOON, stake some and later receive a MOON-pool reward without any privileged seed mint. |
| Mature or concentrated pools can recover much of their trading cost | Model a trader who is also a maker, project creator, large LP or dominant staker. A burn is an incentive to the whole pool and never a personal right to remint. Do not use gross volume to mint an additional subsidy. |
| Launchpad rewards have two funding sources | The initial 300M budget supplies ten eligible years of scheduled rewards without any burns. Curve and AMM trading burns plus founder or community token uses can extend issuance. Track and report both sources separately. |
| Independent release and verification | The arithmetic review checked curve graduation, 40 AMM buy/sell cases across four token precisions, 500 burn/reissue transitions, and 100 paired-MOON release batches. Per-asset conservation, the reserve product and the supply identity held in these model checks. Before mainnet, run actual Soroban WASM, fuzz asset precisions, simultaneous claims, expiry, catch-up, uneven small amounts, grant expiry and fee version changes. Measure resource limits on both swap directions and the heaviest epoch rollover. Independent economic and contract review remains a release gate. |

**Owner decisions still open.** Set the Foundation’s opening STRN ask price or price ladder, any staging, custody addresses and sale-proceeds policy. Community demand determines filled volume and when two-sided liquidity becomes available. Confirm the proposed external-pool 60% LP residual and input-asset settlement, LP fee denomination, initial STRN and MOON release horizons, 30-day recycling/carryover, stake-power parameters, creator payout policy, operating funds, signers and deployment IDs.
